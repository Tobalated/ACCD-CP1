Pattern, Rhythm & Movement — Documentation

This sketch explores tiling, rhythm, HSB color, and complex non-linear motion using p5.js. The grid of rectangles animates using a combination of polar coordinates, Perlin noise, and layered sine waves.

Process: Problems & Solutions

  • Rhythmic structure: I used a grid plus radial coordinates (distCenter, angle) to keep the pattern unified.
  • Symmetry vs. variation: Symmetry comes from the math; variation comes from noise and time-based modulation.
  • Non-linear movement: I combined noise() with multiple sin()/cos() layers for smooth, curved motion.
  • Color control: Hue is tied to angle and time; saturation and brightness depend on distance and a wave function.

Reflection

One of the biggest challenges was making the motion feel complex and non-linear without looking chaotic or jittery. Early versions that used random() each frame produced uncomfortable, noisy movement.

The solution was to rely on noise() for smooth variation, and to tie all motion back to a shared time variable and polar coordinates. This keeps the grid “breathing” together while each tile still follows its own slightly different path.
