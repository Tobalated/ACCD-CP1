Programming Design Systems – “Introduction”

Source: programmingdesignsystems.com/introduction

“We are now in transition from an object-oriented to a systems-oriented culture. Here change emanates, not from things, but from the way things are done.”
— Jack Burnham, Systems Esthetics (1968)

A perfect frame for contemporary design: outcomes emerge from relationships and rules, not isolated artifacts. For UI/brand work, this moves us from polishing screens to shaping behaviors, flows, and lifecycle states across contexts.

Systems thinking
“Even in web design … it seems limiting to define the design to just the styles of the page.”
— Rune Madsen, Introduction

Style isn’t the product; experience is. This nudges designers to validate tasks, feedback loops, and performance under real constraints—then let visual polish serve those systemic goals.

Beyond visuals
“Systems can cure the fear of the blank canvas.”
— Rune Madsen, Introduction

Constraints liberate. Thoughtful design systems (tokens, grids, motion rules) accelerate exploration and keep work coherent as it scales to multiple screens and states.

Creative constraints

What Do Prototypes Prototype? — Houde & Hill

Source (PDF): hci.stanford.edu/.../WhatDoPrototypesPrototype.pdf

“Choosing the right kind of focused prototype is an art in itself.”
— Houde & Hill

Prototyping starts with a question, not a tool. When we articulate what we need to learn (role, look-and-feel, or implementation), we save time and get sharper feedback.

Prototype intent
“By focusing on the purpose of the prototype … we can make better decisions about the kinds of prototypes to build.”
— Houde & Hill

Purpose acts like a north star: if we’re testing comprehension, a clickable storyboard may beat a coded demo. If we’re testing feasibility, a technical spike wins.

Decision making
“Prototypes are not self-explanatory: looks can be deceiving.”
— Houde & Hill

Stakeholders often equate polish with readiness. Labeling scope and known-unknowns (“visual only,” “performance TBD,” “fake data”) prevents false confidence and misaligned expectations.
