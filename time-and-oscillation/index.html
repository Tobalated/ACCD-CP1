Christmas Tree Motion Rhythm – p5.js Animation

This project began with a simple moment at home: decorating my Christmas tree. As I unpacked ornaments, lights, and branches, I noticed how everything moved — the way ornaments gently swung, the lights flickered, and the branches bent. That observation became the foundation for this p5.js sketch, where a Christmas tree comes to life through motion, timing, and noise.

Project Story / Artist Statement

Every year, as Christmas approaches, I pull out a familiar storage box from the locker in my home. The one filled with ornaments, ribbons, lights, and the artificial pine tree I’ve used for several seasons. It’s a small ritual, but it always sets the tone for the holiday spirit.

This year, while I was assembling the tree and placing the ornaments, I caught myself paying more attention than usual to the motion around me. The ornaments swayed when I brushed past them, the lights seemed to flicker with their own rhythm, and the branches shifted slightly under the weight of each new decoration. At the top of the tree, the gold star felt like a small focal point for all of this energy, almost like it was spinning in my imagination.

At one point, I paused to take a picture of myself decorating the tree. Later, looking at that photo, it clicked: this could be the concept for my animation project. Instead of inventing an abstract idea, I could translate this real moment into code, turning the Christmas tree into a system of moving parts: a rotating star, rows of ornaments climbing up the branches, swinging motions, a timed falling ornament, twinkling lights, and drifting snow.

That is how this Christmas Tree Motion Rhythm sketch was born. A blend of holiday tradition and computational design, where everyday movements are transformed into a rhythmic, animated scene.

Embedded p5.js Sketch

Below is the live p5.js sketch hosted on the p5.js Web Editor. Press the number keys 1–6 to switch between the internal iteration states of the animation (from simple tree to full scene). The final version features a rotating gold star, ornaments placed along the edges and up toward the top of the tree, a delayed falling ornament, and noise-driven lights and snow.
